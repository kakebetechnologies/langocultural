Explore Lango: The 9 Districts of Heritage & Adventure

Discover each district through culture, tourism, what to expect, and key economic activities.

Map of the Lango Districts

1) Lira City & Lira District (The Vibrant Hub) ▼

Population (2024): ~487,348

What to Expect: Urban energy, busy trade centers, and strong cultural institutions.

Main Economic Activities: Trade, services, agro-processing, education, and transport logistics.

Context: Lira is the commercial and administrative heartbeat of Lango and a gateway to northern Uganda.

2) Apac District (Land of the Marabou Stork) ▶

Population (2024): ~221,962

What to Expect: Quiet agrarian settings with historical and ecological interest points.

Main Economic Activities: Farming, livestock, fishing pockets, and local produce trade.

Context: Apac blends colonial-era memory with strong traditional performance culture.

3) Oyam District (The Northern Guardian) ▶

Population (2024): ~477,464

What to Expect: Larger settlements, strategic road links, and rich cultural performance traditions.

Main Economic Activities: Crop farming, livestock, transport services, and local commerce.

Context: Oyam is one of the largest contributors to labor, food, and inter-district trade movement.

4) Amolatar District (The Peninsula) ▶

Population (2024): ~188,715

What to Expect: Lakeside breeze, fresh fish, and a calm tourism environment.

Main Economic Activities: Fishing, boat services, farming, and emerging lake tourism.

Context: Amolatar’s position around Lake Kyoga/Kwania gives it a distinct blue-economy identity.

5) Otuke District (The Ancestral Cradle) ▶

Population (2024): ~161,069

What to Expect: Historic migration memory sites and rugged natural scenery.

Main Economic Activities: Shea products, crop farming, livestock, and community tourism.

Context: Otuke is revered for ancestral origins and deep cultural symbolism in Lango history.

6) Dokolo District (The Crossroads) ▶

Population (2024): ~215,625

What to Expect: Productive plains, cross-regional movement, and vibrant music culture.

Main Economic Activities: Farming, livestock trade, produce markets, and transport services.

Context: Dokolo connects Lango to eastern neighbors and supports inter-regional market flow.

7) Kole District (The Food Basket) ▶

Population (2024): ~294,301

What to Expect: Green agricultural scenery and strong homestead-based cultural life.

Main Economic Activities: Food crop production, citrus, local markets, and agro-supply chains.

Context: Kole supplies a significant share of food to Lira and neighboring districts.

8) Alebtong District (The Eastern Gate) ▶

Population (2024): ~283,509

What to Expect: Mixed cultural interactions, historic sites, and resilient local communities.

Main Economic Activities: Crop farming, petty trade, artisan work, and livestock keeping.

Context: Alebtong sits at a cultural transition zone with strong clan traditions and social cohesion.

9) Kwania District (The Lake-Side Frontier) ▶

Population (2024): ~216,125

What to Expect: Lakeside development, fishing communities, and scenic hill viewpoints.

Main Economic Activities: Fishing, boat transport, agriculture, and local fish trade.

Context: Kwania is a fast-growing district with high potential in lake tourism and blue-economy investments.
